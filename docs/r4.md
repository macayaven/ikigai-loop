I ran a sensitivity test, not proof of faithfulness: I changed the value of one cited fact per case against a matched control change, and I compare first-attempt drafts before the gate.

- pick: 5/10 cited vs 1/10 control -- held
- whole signature: 10/10 cited vs 10/10 control -- not held -- no differential sensitivity detected

Changed pick means the regenerated pick differs from the base pick. Changed whole signature means the multiset of (section, sorted fact ids, sorted metric refs) over overall_state, working and not_working differs. Changed reason signature means the first overall_state claim's (sorted fact ids, sorted metric refs) differs. None of the three can fire on new digits or rewording alone. I read equal rates with care: they can come from changes my signatures are insensitive to, from relevant controls that move with the cited change, or from generation noise between draws, so equal rates don't show what citations do.

## Method

I regenerate each arm once, first attempt only, through TimeoutRetryClient around LocalRetryClient around MuseExecClient at the 120 s timeout in Standard mode, with 0 timeout retries, with the same prompt and schema builders propose calls. I pass an empty policy block and no parent, so each draft stands on its scenario alone. Store-derived literals are empty by construction and I pass private terms empty and available, so the private-term check is not exercised by these rows. No gate runs on these drafts: the comparison is before the gate. Re-running R4 on a new gate first needs R3 on that gate, since cases inherit R3's gate.

A claim that flips direction in words while keeping identical citations counts as unchanged; that is the price of a mechanical, pre-registered reading, and the noise floor above calibrates how often each signature moves on its own. Changing one flat fact to 0 makes it the only change in a flat scenario, so a control can gain citations for that reason alone; the cites-changed column shows that and decides nothing.
