## Rule

Widened in review: we do not know which arm fired on the real term, and it may have been a single-token fuzzy arm rather than a join. So the vocabulary rule covers every fuzzy arm of the private-term matching, not only the joins: a fuzzy or squashed match does not fire when every token it consumes is a card-vocabulary word. An exact whole-term match of a private term, the term as written found in the text, still fires, vocabulary or not.

The vocabulary is the card's own claim vocabulary: the gate's closed word lists (the period words at [claim_text.py:340](../../framework/briefing/claim_text.py:340), the up/down/same/steady words, the comparison vocabulary at [claim_text.py:408](../../framework/briefing/claim_text.py:408), the English stopwords at [claim_text.py:843](../../framework/briefing/claim_text.py:843)), the unit words `hour` and `hours`, number tokens from the text being checked, and every label form of the metrics in the card's snapshot. It holds every label form because different wordings tripping at the same claims point at what the data supplies to those claims: label words and numbers. A number token counts as vocabulary in every form an arm consumes it, a digit-for-letter reading of it included; only pure-digit runs count as number tokens, so a digit-mixed spelling stays a spelling and the digit-for-letter arm keeps catching it. The vocabulary is the card's own because those are the words a true card cannot avoid: period, direction, comparison and function words, units, digits, and the names of the metrics the card cites. A reader meeting "lower in the last period" does not read a private term there.

Everything else still counts: a match inside one word; a term spelled letter by letter, whatever the separators (spaces, dots, dashes); and a fuzzy match consuming a word outside that vocabulary, which is what keeps "Zebra Finch", "Zebra; finch", "Zebra, finch", "Zebra-finch" and "quetzal: garden" stopped. A letter-by-letter spelling is never exempt even when its pieces are single characters: every covered word a single character reads as spelling, not wording. This ticket may only narrow the private-term rule: no new firing path. With an empty vocabulary every arm returns exactly what it returned before this ticket, and the vocabulary can only discard a match, never add one; the proof test pins that over every private-term sentence in the repository. The rule is general, for every claim at every slot, not for particular claim locations.

## Arms

- The squashed matcher in `gate.py` that `text_check_codes` uses ([gate.py:114](../../framework/briefing/gate.py:114)): takes the vocabulary and skips an occurrence whose span covers whole words end to end only when every covered word is vocabulary.
- The squashed matcher in `claim_text.py` ([claim_text.py:2364](../../framework/briefing/claim_text.py:2364)), both where it decides a private-term hit ([claim_text.py:2490](../../framework/briefing/claim_text.py:2490)) and where it decides that the squashed text holds a term ([claim_text.py:3037](../../framework/briefing/claim_text.py:3037), which gates the spelled-out-letters rule): same skip, same vocabulary.
- The neighbouring-words arms: the near and exact joins of two neighbours and rotation on two joined words skip the pair when both neighbours are vocabulary; rotation, l/i fold, edit distance 1, digit-for-letter, wildcard and reversed-token arms skip when the single consumed claim token is all vocabulary; the reversed-word arm skips when every occurrence of the reversed word covers vocabulary words only.
- Grep for any other arm that joins whole words (`itertools.pairwise`, `pair`, `neighbour`, `rotation` over `framework/`) finds no other: `_sequence_hit` and the raw-literal bag arm match forbidden raw literals, not private terms, and the exact whole-term match (`norm_term in lower`) still fires unconditionally, vocabulary or not.
- Supply: `claim_text_codes` builds the vocabulary from the snapshot metric ids it already receives, expanding every binding label through `label_forms` and adding number tokens from the claim; the gate's `_l1_text_checks` builds it from its context's snapshot the same way; `text_check_codes` takes the label words as the optional keyword `label_words` and keeps working for callers that pass none (closed lists plus number tokens, the stricter verdict); `scan_outbound_text` passes the new argument through. The public checker reuses `text_check_codes`, so [core.py](../../framework/verify/core.py) passes the cited metrics' declared label words at its one call site and stays in step; the hardened checker inherits it through `verify_claims`.

## Limits

- A join of ordinary words outside that vocabulary, in a fact-only claim or a reason clause, can still be flagged. The exemption covers only the card's own closed lists, units, numbers and snapshot labels; any other wording that happens to spell a private term across a boundary still stops the card.
- A private term made only of vocabulary words isn't caught when written as separate words, which I accept because a reader doesn't read a private term there. A term written as it stands (a direct substring, a hyphenated compound, a masked or leet spelling inside one word) is still caught.
- The exemption can only discard matches, never add one: with an empty vocabulary every arm behaves exactly as before this ticket, pinned by the monotonicity proof test over the repository's whole private-term corpus (unit-test rows, red-team rounds, true-sentence files, mutants). The check is location-general: no arm consults the section or claim id.
